An estimated 1 in 8 Americans living with HIV do not know their status.

Emergency departments serve the communities HIV efforts most need to reach. Despite this, only about 1 in 100 ED visits includes an HIV test. The HIVer Project partners with EDs, health systems, and health departments to build the data to show where routine testing should go next, state by state, with the goal of helping end the HIV epidemic in the U.S.

EDs are everywhere. The HIV burden is not.

Our state projects

The HIVer Project’s goal is national: to understand where the burden of HIV is highest. Knowing this can help guide testing, treatment and prevention so they reach the people living with HIV and those at risk.

We start in emergency departments, where many people living with or at risk of HIV get their care. Our state projects build this picture one state at a time.

HIVer Project

This work is conducted through the HIVer Research Program, a multi-institutional research program that aims to improve HIV testing, diagnosis, and linkage to care in U.S. emergency departments. Work is based out of the Bennett Lab in the Department of Emergency Medicine at the Stanford University School of Medicine. bennettlab@stanford.edu
